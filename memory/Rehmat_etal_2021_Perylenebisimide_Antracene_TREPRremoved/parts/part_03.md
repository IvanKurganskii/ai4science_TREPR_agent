Femtosecond transient absorption spectroscopy
To elucidate the kinetics of the photophysical processes, femtosecond transient absorption measurements on PBI-AN
dyads/triad were carried out (Figure 7). Ultrafast pump–probe
experiments for PBI-AN dyads/triad were performed in toluene
at 500 nm excitation, a wavelength which is predominantly
resonant with the excitation of the PBI chromophore.
On photoexcitation of the PB-2-AN dyad, the singlet excited
state of the PBI chromophore is populated, as is evident by
the appearance of GSB centered at 540 nm, stimulated emission (SE) in the range of 585–650 nm, and a broad ESA band
centered at 740 nm (Figure 7 a). Within a few picoseconds
(10 ps), the SE disappears, the ESA band at 740 nm further
rises in intensity, and also a broad ESA band centered at 675
appears, which can be assigned to a charge-transfer process.
The charge transfer appears to be a biexponential process, as
at further long time delay (51 ps), an additional rise in the intensity of ESA band was observed. The charge recombination
process takes place on a longer timescale in the range of few
nanoseconds, and the triplet excited state of PBI is populated.
A similar photophysical process was observed in PBI-Bis-2-AN

with slow charge separation, which takes place within 60 ps
(Figure S30 in the Supporting Information).
In the case of PBI-9-AN, an intense GSB band centered at
515 nm, stimulated emission in the range of 550–640 nm, and
a broad ESA band in the range of 600–750 nm were observed
on photoexcitation (Figure S31 in the Supporting Information).
Relatively fast charge separation was observed in case of PBI9-AN compared to PBI-2-AN, which is also in line with its low
fluorescence quantum yield and high ISC ability. The electrochemical analysis also confirmed the strong PET process in
PBI-9-AN, which results in lowering the CSS level.
To obtain the exact rate constant of charge separation, a
global analysis of transient absorption data was performed, retrieving the evolution associated difference spectra. In the case
of PBI-2-AN, the singlet excited state decays within 3.6 ps due
to charge separation as the SE band disappears and new positive absorption band starts rising (evolution from black to red
line in Figure 7 b). The positive absorption band in the range
of 650–700 nm further rises in intensity, and a slight increase in
GSB was also observed in the evolution from red to blue occurring at 47.6 ps, which indicates that the charge transfer is
apparently a biexponential process. The CR process takes place
on a longer timescale (2.8 ns) and populates the long-lived PBI
triplet state (evolution from blue to green). In the case of PBIBis-2-AN triad, the singlet state decays within 9.2 ps and CSS
appears and further rises in intensity till 509 ps (Figure S30 in
the Supporting Information), while in the case of PBI-9-AN, a
fast CSS process was observed that takes place within 1.48 ps
(Figure S31 in the Supporting Information).
