To rationalize the photophysical properties, the ground-state
geometries of the dyads/triad were optimized by DFT calculations. The frontier molecular orbitals of the PBI-AN dyads/triad
are shown in Scheme 2.
In the case of PBI-2-AN and PBI-Bis-2-AN, the AN and PBI
moieties are not completely decoupled and the dihedral
angles are significantly reduced (Figure 9). For PBI-2-AN, the
observed dihedral angle between donor and acceptor moieties
is approximately 558. Frontier molecular orbital also show orbital overlap of donor and acceptor moieties to some extent. For
PBI-Bis-2-AN, similar dihedral angles between the PBI and the
AN moieties were observed (ca. 598). Due to conformational
restriction, the dihedral angle in PBI-9-AN is 768, close to orthogonal geometry, which is beneficial for SOCT-ISC. Due to
this electronic coupling and extended p conjugation between
AN and PBI moieties, redshifted absorption spectra were observed in PBI-2-AN (Figure 2 a). In all these derivatives, the PBI
moiety is not planar and show twisting to some extent, which
is common feature of bay-substituted PBI.[87, 88] The PBI-AN
dyads PBI-2-AN and PBI-9-AN show slightly larger core twisting angle (148 and 178, respectively) as compared to triad PBIBis-2-AN, in which the PBI core is twisted by 128. Previously,
enhanced ISC was observed for some PBI derivatives with
twisted molecular structures.[89, 90] Torsion of the PBI core does
not necessarily induce efficient ISC; for instance, previously we

Photochemical studies: reversible photoreduction to produce the radical anion (PBIC@) and dianion (PBI2@) in the
presence of sacrificial electron donor triethanolamine
To explore the potential application of the dyads/triad in
photo-driven electron transfer, an elementary process in photocatalysis, we conducted the photoreduction of the dyads in
presence of a sacrificial electron donor (PBI-9-AN, Figure 11).
The UV/Vis absorption changes of the dyads on photoirradiation in the presence of sacrificial electron donor triethanolamine (TEOA) were monitored (Figure 11 a).

lamp in the presence of TEOA, the absorption bands of PBI-9An at 512 nm decreased, and new absorption bands centered

are the characteristic D0 !Dn absorption of PBIC@ .[91–93] Notably,
the absorption band centered at 369 nm (AN absorption) do
not change, that is, the AN moiety remains intact during photoreduction. This implies that PBIC@ is formed, due to photoreduction of the dyad in the presence of TEOA. Other PBI-AN de-

After the photoreduction, the PBIC@ solution was exposed to
air, the PBIC@ absorption bands centered at 708, 802, 864, and
954 nm decreased, and the absorption band of the neutral PBI
moiety at 512 nm fully recovered (Figure 11 b). These results indicated oxidation of PBIC@ by the O2 in air to produce the neutral form of the compound. Radical formation was reproduced

significant decomposition, which indicates excellent reversibility of formation of PBIC@ (Figure S33 in the Supporting Information). The orange color of PBI-9-AN changes to colorless on
radical-anion formation and the original color of the compound recovered on exposure of the photoirradiated solution
to air (Figure 11 c). A similar phenomenon was observed in the
case of dyad PBI-2-AN (Figure S32 in the Supporting Information).
Interesting results were obtained for PBI-Bis-2-AN on photoirradiation (Figure 12). On irradiation of the mixed solution
with TEOA for 6 min, the irradiated solution shows reduced ab-

sorption below 600 nm and the appearance of new NIR absorption bands centered at 739, 825, 887, and 999 nm, due to
the formation of PBIC@ . The color of the solution changed from
purple to light gray (Figure 12 c). Interestingly, on further irradiation afterwards with white light, the intensity of the near-IR
absorption bands centered at 715, 808, and 973 nm decreased,
a new band centered at 596 nm appeared along with a minor
shoulder at 545 nm (Figure 12 b), and the color of the solution
changed from light gray to blue (Figure 12 c). This change in
absorption on prolonged irradiation is due to formation of the
PBI dianion (PBIC@ !PBI2@).[92–95] The formation of stable radical
anion and dianion is important, because it has been shown
that photoexcited anions are better potential reductants than
the neutral chromophores.[94.95]
