The fluorescence emission spectra of PBI-AN dyads/triad
were also studied on excitation of the AN unit at 360 nm (Figure S18 in the Supporting Information). In PBI-AN derivatives,
no LE emission of the AN moiety was observed. The quenching of the fluorescence of the AN moiety can be due to Fçrster
resonance energy transfer (FRET), CS, or the ISC of the AN
moiety. To confirm the reason for fluorescence quenching, we
studied the fluorescence excitation spectra of three derivatives
and compared them with the UV/Vis absorption spectra (Figure S23 in the Supporting Information). We found that fluorescence excitation intensity is lower than the UV/Vis absorption
intensity in the short-wavelength region. This is probably because singlet energy-transfer/charge-separation efficiency from
the AN to the PBI moiety is not unity, owing to, for instance,
the ISC of the AN moiety.
We measured the singlet-oxygen quantum yield of PBI-9-AN
in toluene using different excitation wavelengths. We observed
different singlet-oxygen quantum yields on excitation of different units. On 525 nm photoexcitation (excitation of the PBI
unit), 49 % singlet-oxygen quantum yield was observed, while
only 20 % singlet oxygen quantum yield was observed on exciting the AN unit at 355 nm. A possible reason for different
singlet-oxygen quantum yields at different excitation wavelengths may be an inefficient FRET process or the existence of
other decay channels on excitation at short wavelength
(355 nm).

For PBI-Ph, two reversible reduction waves at @1.11 and
@1.33 V (vs. Fc/Fc + ) were observed (Figure 5 a). No reversible
oxidation wave was observed in the potential range used in
the electrochemical measurements. A similar trend was also
observed for PBI-Bis-Ph (Figure 5 b). These results indicate that
the PBI moiety more likely acts as electron acceptor in these
dyads/triad, because the AN moiety shows an oxidation wave
at 0.90 V and it should act as an electron donor. For PBI-9-AN,
two reversible reduction waves at @1.10 and @1.32 V were observed (Figure 5 a), which are similar to those of the PBI
moiety. One reversible oxidation wave at + 0.93 V was also recorded, which can be assigned to the AN moiety. In the case
of dyad PBI-2-AN, two irreversible oxidation waves at + 0.92
and + 1.02 V were observed, which can be assigned to the AN
moiety. Two reversible reduction waves similar to that of the
PBI moiety at @1.09 and @1.29 V were also observed. Similar
results were observed for PBI-Bis-2-AN (Table 3).
