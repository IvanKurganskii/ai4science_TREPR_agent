In order to determine the kinetics of the ISC, sub-nanosecond
transient absorption measurement was performed for the BDPPBI derivatives (Figure 7). Upon selective excitation of BDP unit
in bay substituted PBI derivatives, ultrafast CS (< 10 ps) was
observed, as shown by the fs-TA spectra (Figure 6 and Figure
S17). The formation of PBI radical anion (PBI−•), indicated by the
sharp positive absorption bands centered at 720 nm and 806 nm,
was confirmed and is also consistent with PBI−• absorption
obtained by chemical reduction method (see later section). At
longer time delay, the absorption bands belonging to the PBI−•
decays and new absorption bands at 470 nm and 580 nm rise
gradually, which are assigned to the ESA band of the triplet state
of the PBI moiety (T1Tn transition).[6567] ISC rate was found to
be 6.8 ns and 6.0 ns for BDP-PBI-1 and BDP-PBI-2, respectively,
by monitoring the growth of the ESA band at 470 nm.
For BDP-PBI-3 (Figure 7c), upon excitation the PBI
chromophore, singlet excited state appears firstly, then within 1.5
ns, the CS occurs, as confirmed by the fs-TA studies (Figure S19).
Interestingly, the CR for BDP-PBI-3 is quite slow, which takes 21
ns (Figure 7d). The CR leads to the population of the upper triplet
state, i.e. 3BDP*, supported by the broad ESA band centered at
ca. 440 nm, which is the typical 3BDP* state absorption.[18, 68, 69]
The BDP triplet decays at slow timescale, as confirmed in the later
section by the nanosecond to microsecond transient absorption
measurements. To the best of our knowledge, this is an rare
experimental evidence that the upper triplet state is involved in
SOCT-ISC.

To unravel the photophysical process and obtain the direct
information of the different species and the kinetics explicitly,
global analysis of the ns-TA spectra data was performed (Figure
9b). Two triplet species with apparent lifetimes of 14.5 μs and
161.2 μs were obtained, which belongs to BDP and PBI triplet
states, respectively. The decay curve at 470 nm was also
monitored for the evolution of the transient species of BDP-PBI3, a biphasic kinetic trace was observed that contains a rise phase,
taking 14.0 μs, and a decay phase taking 118.9 μs (Figure 9c).
The rise phase is attributed to the intramolecular TTET, and the
second phase is attributed to the decay of the PBI triplet state.
TTET is usually occur via Dexter mechanism, the weak electronic
coupling between the BDP and PBI moiety may render the slow
intramolecular TTET in BDP-PBI-3.
To exclude the possibility of intermolecular character of the
TTET process for the slow kinetics of BDP-PBI-3, i.e. via
diffusion-controlled process, the ns-TA spectra of the BDP-PBI-3
were also carried out in viscous solvent (Figure S23), i.e. soybean
oil (viscosity = 57.1 mPas, in comparison, the viscosity is 0.59
mPas for toluene. 20 °C). Similar rising phase in the evolution of
the transient absorption kinetic trace at 470 nm, taking 22.4 s,
was observed, as that in fluid solvent toluene (14 s). The decay
phase takes much longer time (255.2 s) than that in toluene
(118.9 s). This is clear evidence that the TTET is an
intramolecular process. It is known that the triplet state lifetime
can be shorten by triplet-triplet annihilation (TTA) effect.[75] The
transient absorption measurements of the BDP-PBI-3 were also
carried out in chloroform, similar, but strong triplet signal was
recorded which is in agreement with its high triplet yield in
chloroform.
