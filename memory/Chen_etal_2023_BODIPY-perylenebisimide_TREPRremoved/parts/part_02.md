The fluorescence of BDP-PBI-1 and BDP-PBI-3 were
compared upon normalization (Figure 3b). In case of BDP-PBI-1,
two emission bands were observed, i.e., a narrow band at 514 nm
which can be assigned to the BDP chromophore while a broad
emission band at 549 nm belongs to the PBI moiety. While for
BDP-PBI-3, characteristic emission profile of PBI chromophore
was observed, no BDP emission appeared. The result indicates
that in BDP-PBI-3, the Fӧrster resonance energy transfer (FRET)
is very efficient. The solvent-dependent fluorescence of BDP-PBI
dyads/triad was also studied (Figure S14). Upon increasing the
solvent polarity, the fluorescence of BDP-PBI dyads/triad is

progressively quenched, which support the PET process in these
derivatives.
The fluorescence emission decay traces of BDP-PBI
dyads/triad along with reference compounds were also studied in
toluene using time correlated single photon counting (TCSPC)
detection technique (Figure 4). The fluorescence emission of all
the BDP-PBI dyads/triad decay following a biexponential feature,
while in case of reference compounds mono-exponential
fluorescence decay traces were observed. In case of BDP-PBI-3,
the biexponential decay showed a fast component (1.8 ns,
population ratio: 95.7%) and a slow component (15.2 ns,
population ratio: 4.3%. Figure 4c). The shorting of the
fluorescence lifetimes of the energy acceptor infers electron
transfer.
The fluorescence lifetime of BDP-PBI-1 and BDP-PBI-2 was
measured at two emission bands corresponding to the BDP and
PBI unit (Figure 4 and Figure S15). At PBI emission band, both
dyad (BDP-PBI-1) and triad (BDP-PBI-2) showed a biexponential
decay feature (Figure 4a and 4b). BDP-PBI-1 shows lifetime of
2.8 ns (population ratio: 94.4%) and 7.8 ns (5.6%) while in case
of BDP-PBI-2, the fast component decays with a lifetime of 2.6 ns
(97.6%) and slow component lifetime is 7.9 ns (2.4%).

Interestingly, monoexponential decay traces were observed at
BDP emission band for both BDP-PBI-1 and BDP-PBI-2, with
lifetime of 2.9 ns and 3.2 ns, respectively (Figure S15). Whereas
the reference compounds R-1, R-2 and R-3 show lifetime of 6.0
ns, 7.3 ns and 4.4 ns, respectively (Figure 4). [50] The quenched
and biexponential feature of fluorescence lifetime in BDP-PBI
dyads/triad as compared to reference compounds is an indication
of PET process in these derivatives.
The detailed photophysical properties of the BDP-PBI
dyads/triad along with reference compounds are compiled in
Table 1. To confirm the ISC capability of BDP-PBI dyads/triad, the
singlet oxygen quantum yield () of the compounds was
measured. For BDP-PBI-3,  up to 85% (in chloroform) and 40%
(in toluene) was observed. For BDP-PBI-1 and BDP-PBI-2,
moderate   of 30% and 27%, respectively, were observed (in
toluene). The reference compounds R-1 and R-2 exhibited 
values of only 15% and 6%, respectively (in toluene). The results
indicate that the ISC capability of dyads BDP-PBI-1 and BDPPBI-2 are caused by the presence of the BDP chromophore, for
instance by the charge recombination induced-ISC. Meanwhile
negligible 1O2 was observed for R-3. Moreover, in polar solvents,
no 1O2 generation was observed by BDP-PBI derivatives. This
solvent dependent 1O2 generation ability of these derivatives
supports the SOCT-ISC mechanism of triplet state population,
and the ISC of the dyads and triad is not caused by the twisted
PBI chromophore.[5660]

Moreover, from the ΔG0CS values, it is obvious that the PET
process is comparatively stronger in BDP-PBI-1 and BDP-PBI-2
as compared to BDP-PBI-3, which is in accordance with strong
fluorescence quenching in the bay-substituted donor-acceptor
derivatives. Hence on the basis of fluorescence emission and
electrochemical studies; we can claim that the PET is more
efficient in the bay-substituted PBI donor-acceptor system as
compared to imide substituted PBI donor-acceptor system. This
assumption will be further verified by femtosecond transient
absorption studies and theoretical computations in the later
section.
The charge separated state energy levels (ECSS) of the dyads
were calculated (Table 3). The ECSS values are similar for all the
compounds in a specific solvent, but the ECSS energy level
decreases from that in non-polar solvent to polar solvents.
Moreover, since the T1 state of the PBI chromophore is 1.2 eV,
thus, we expect a 3LE triplet state should be observed for all the
dyads, not a CS state.

BDP-PBI derivatives were measured, using the 500 nm excitation,
which predominantly excite the BDP unit.
