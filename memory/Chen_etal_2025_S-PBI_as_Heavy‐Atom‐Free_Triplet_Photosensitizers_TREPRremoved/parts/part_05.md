Previously the photoexcited anions or dianions were studied
as strong reductants, however, the lifetimes of the corresponding excited doublet states (for monoanion, in ps range) or
the excited singlet state (for dianion, in ns range) are too
short for efficient intermolecular electron transfer. Thus, these
long-lived photoexcited triplet states of the electron-abundant
dianions can be used as efficient super strong reductant
in photocatalytic redox organic reactions. Improving water
solubility of the investigated molecules is one of the objectives
of the future research as this will be beneficial for various
in vivo applications. This can be done, e.g., via polar group
modification or structure optimization of the photosensitizers.
The material’s performance can be validated on small animals,
including assessing the distribution of photosensitizers, their
biocompatibility, and therapeutic efficacy in tumor models,
paving the way for practical use of heavy-atom-free triplet
photosensitizers in the future. Our present results should
inspire future design of new triplet photosensitizer as well as
in-depth study of the photophysics and electron spin dynamics
of the ISC and triplet excited states of these compounds.
