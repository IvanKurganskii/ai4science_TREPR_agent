DFT calculations
In order to rationalize the photophysical properties, the ground
state geometries of the perylenebisimide-carbazole (PBI–Cz)
dyads were optimized with the DFT method by using the
CAM-B3LYP/6-31G(d) basis set.
The frontier molecular orbitals of the dyads are presented
(Scheme 2). For all three dyads, the HOMO is mainly localized
on the Cz moiety, and the LUMO is localized on the PBI moiety.
This result indicates the possibility of the electron transfer
from Cz to the PBI moiety in the dyads upon photoexcitation.
The spatial separation of the HOMO and LUMO orbitals also
account for the dark feature of the 1CT state. Moreover, the
electron density of the HOMO also spreads to the intervening
phenyl moieties in all the dyads, but to a slightly lesser extent in
PBI-Cz-2 compared to other dyads. In principle the separation

The photophysical processes of the dyads upon photoexcitation are summarized in Scheme 3. PBI is a visible light-harvesting
chromophore, and upon photoexcitation, ultrafast CS occurs
(supported by the quenched fluorescence of the PBI moiety and
the fast decay of the LE emission), and the CT state is formed. The
CT states of the current dyads are dark states, i.e. non-emissive.
The subsequent CR will lead to the formation of 3LE states. Since
the CT state energy level decreases in polar solvents, the matching
of the 1CT state and the Tn state may change, and as a result, the
ISC eﬃciency is highly solvent polarity-dependent. The singlet
oxygen quantum yields are high in toluene (Table 1), but the
singlet oxygen quantum yields in polar solvents are much lower.
Another possible reason for ineﬃcient triplet population in high
polar solvents can be the fast CR process producing the S0 state
(CR is in the Marcus inverted region of electron transfer), which
competes strongly with SOCT-ISC. The Jablonski diagrams show
the photophysical processes involved in the other two dyads (PBICz-1 and PBI-Cz-2, see the ESI†).

be noted that dyads (PBI-Cz-1 and PBI-Cz-3) with one phenyl

different from the previously reported molecular structural
profiles which require an orthogonal geometry for efficient
SOCT-ISC. Time-resolved electron paramagnetic resonance (TREPR)
spectroscopy reveals that all three dyads have the same electron
spin polarization of eae/aea, which is different from the SO-ISC
(eee/aaa), thus confirming the SOCT–ISC mechanism. Moreover, upon photoirradiation, PBI-Cz-1 and PBI-Cz-3 show the
formation of the near-IR absorbing PBI radical anion (PBI ) in
the presence of the sacrificial electron donor (TEOA). These
results are useful for future design of heavy atom-free triplet
photosensitizers and for the study of the charge recombination
in the electron donor/acceptor dyads.

Experimental section
Materials and methods
All chemicals used in the study are of analytical grade and
used as received. UV-Vis absorption spectra were recorded on
a UV-2550 UV-Vis spectrophotometer (Shimadzu Ltd, Japan).
Fluorescence spectra were obtained using a RF-5301PC spectrofluorometer (Shimadzu Ltd, Japan). The fluorescence lifetimes
were measured using an OB920 luminescence lifetime spectrometer (Edinburgh Instruments Ltd, UK).
