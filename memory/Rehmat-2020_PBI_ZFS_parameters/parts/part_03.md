Time-resolved EPR spectral study
In order to study the triplet state properties and the ISC
mechanisms of the dyads, time-resolved electron paramagnetic
resonance (TR-EPR) spectra of the dyads were recorded. Based
on the UV-Vis absorption spectrum (Fig. 1a), we acquired the
TR-EPR spectra of the compounds (Fig. 5) upon photoexcitation

at 355 and 530–536 nm, where the Cz (355 nm) and/or the PBI
(at 530 ca. and 355 nm) moieties show absorption maxima,
respectively.
Upon pulsed photoexcitation at 530–536 nm, all the dyads
show similar TREPR spectra, with eae/aea electron spin polarization (ESP, a and e refer to enhanced absorption or emission,
respectively). On the basis of the best-fit ZFS parameters,
D = ca. 46 mT and E = ca. 4 mT (Tables 4 and 5), the triplet
state is localized on the PBI moiety for all three dyads.
Polarization is not a typical triplet state accessed by a regular
SO-ISC,42 for its polarization would be eee/aaa (see the spectrum labeled PBI-ISC in Fig. 5a, simulated with the parameters
taken from ref. 42 and shown in Tables 4 and 5). The spectral
features are, instead, rather similar to those for compact PBI–
PTZ dyads, where the triplet was obtained by SOCT-ISC.42
Moreover, similar ESP patterns were observed in the case of
JulolidineAnthracene (DMJ-AN) dyads exhibiting SOCT-ISC.46

For this type of ISC process, we still have a population of the
ZFS states, but the population rates of the sublevels of the
triplet state are different from those obtained from a regular
SO-ISC. The population of the ZFS states (molecular frame) will
be carried over to the high-field energy levels (laboratory
frame), and thus the highly polarized TREPR spectra of the
triplet state were observed. It should be pointed out that
the typical ESP of the triplet state accessed with RP ISC (either
aee/aae or eaa/eea42) was not observed, and we didn’t observe
any narrow bands relative to the spin correlated radical pair
(SC RP) species, and thus, the ISC of the dyads discussed in this
manuscript is not due to RP ISC.67 The TR-EPR spectra of the
dyads were also recorded upon excitation at 355 nm (Fig. 5b).
PBI-Cz-1 and PBI-Cz-3 exhibited similar spectra (Fig. 5b), and
both showed the formation of two triplet states, localized on
the PBI and the Cz moiety, respectively. Instead, PBI-Cz-2 leads
to a spectrum in which only the Cz triplet is present. Also for
this species, polarization is different from that obtained from a
regular SO-ISC of carbazole (Table 4).68 This result indicates
that the intramolecular triplet energy transfer is retarded at low
temperature.69 Notably, PBI-Cz-2 has two intervening phenyl
moieties between the PBI and the Cz moieties, yet no RP ISC
was observed for this dyad, based on the ESP of the triplet state
of the TREPR spectrum. Interestingly, for the previously reported
PBI-Ph2-PTZ dyad, RP-ISC contributes significantly above 150 K
(at low temperature, the SO-ISC contributes more, accordingly the
ESP changed from aee/aae to eee/aaa with decreasing temperature).42
Another interesting result is that the tilting of the ZFS principal
axis of the PBI and the Cz moieties in the three dyads is
different, yet the ESP of the triplet state of the PBI moiety
formed via the SOCT-ISC is the same for the three dyads. This
result is different from the previous report that the ESP is
sensitive to the mutual orientation of the electron donor and
acceptor in SOCT-ISC electron donor/acceptor dyads.53,69

are different, and for PBI-Phn-PTZ (n = 2–5), the X and
Y sublevels are almost equally populated. For PBI-Ph1-PTZ,

Photochemical studies: reversible formation of a radical anion
in the presence of the sacrificial electron donor TEOA
Photoreduction experiments were conducted in order to confirm the potential application of the dyads in photocatalysis
(Fig. 6). The PBI–Cz dyads show efficient ISC, and thus these
compounds can be potentially used as photocatalysts to derive
electron transfer.41 The UV-Vis absorption changes of the dyads
were monitored upon photo-irradiation using a xenon lamp in
the presence of TEOA in acetonitrile. Upon photo-irradiation,
PBI-Cz-1 and PBI-Cz-3 show the formation of the PBI radical
anion (PBI ) absorption bands at 698 nm, 794 nm and
953 nm, in the presence of sacrificial electron donor triethanolamine (TEOA).70–72 The emergence of new absorption bands
at 698 nm, 794 nm and 953 nm indicates the characteristic
D0 - Dn absorption of the PBI radical anion (PBI ).70–72
For dyad PBI-Cz-3, the absorption band at 519 nm gradually
decreased in intensity under deaerated conditions in the
presence of TEOA upon photoirradiation. New absorption
bands centred at 698 nm, 794 nm and 953 nm appeared and
there was a gradual increase in intensity with time upon
irradiation (Fig. 6a). These gradually intensifying new NIR
bands indicate the formation of the PBI radical anion (PBI ).
These new NIR absorption bands attain maximum intensity
upon 15 minutes of photoirradiation under a N2 environment
and the absorbance band at 519 nm was reduced to about 66%
