Introduction
Triplet photosensitizers (PSs) are versatile materials for applications in photocatalysis,1–4 photovoltaics,5–7 photodynamic therapy
(PDT),8–15 and triplet–triplet annihilation (TTA) upconversion.16–23
Intersystem crossing (ISC) is the crucial property of triplet PSs.24
The heavy atom effect is a typical method to enhance ISC by
using atoms such as Ir, Ru, Pt, I or Br.25–31 However, concerning
some applications, dark toxicity and the high cost of these heavy

is highly desirable to develop heavy atom-free triplet PSs, which
are also fundamentally interesting in photochemistry studies.
Various ISC mechanisms have been studied, for instance the
above mentioned heavy atom effect, n–p* - p–p* transitions,33
exciton coupling,34–36 systems based on spin converters37–40 and the
energy level matching of Sn/Tn states.24,41 In comparison, the charge
recombination (CR) induced ISC was less investigated. It is known
that the CR in electron donor/acceptor dyads, with long separation
between the donor and the acceptor, may induce ISC, given that the
electron exchange integral is extremely small ( J o 0.1 cm1).42
Under these conditions, radical pair ISC (RP-ISC) may occur by the
magnetic coupling between the electron and the magnetic nuclei, i.e.
ISC of 1CT to 3CT. The subsequent electron spin-selective CR would
produce a locally excited triplet state (3LE), given that the 3LE state
has a lower energy level than the CT state.
It is worth mentioning that the charge separation (CS) and
the CR are electron spin selective, which means 1LE - S0 and

Results and discussion
Molecular design and synthesis
Previously bay-substituted carbazole–perylenebisimide donor–
acceptor systems have been investigated, which show strong
ground state electronic coupling, but no ISC was studied.56
It is considered that the chemical modification at the imide
position will result in weak to moderate electronic coupling
between the donor and acceptor moieties. It is interesting to
investigate the ISC ability of the imide substituted PBI–Cz
donor/acceptor system. For this study, three PBI–Cz dyads were
designed by attaching a carbazole donor at the imide position
of the PBI acceptor. The linker length between the PBI and the
carbazole moiety was varied from one phenyl moiety (PBI-Cz-1
and PBI-Cz-3) to two phenyl moieties (PBI-Cz-2, Scheme 1). The
orientation of the PBI and the carbazole moieties was also varied
to study the relationship between the molecular structure and
the SOCT-ISC efficiency.
UV-Vis absorption and emission
The UV-Vis absorption spectra of the compounds were studied
(Fig. 1a). All three PBI–Cz dyads and the reference compound
PBI-Ph display very similar absorption features. The structured
absorption bands between 400 and 550 nm are assigned to the
S0 - S1 transition of the PBI core. The overlapping spectral
features indicate that the attachment of carbazole on the imide
position of PBI has little influence on the electronic structure of
the PBI core. In other words, no electronic coupling between
carbazole and the PBI moiety was observed in the ground state.

Paper
However, in the case of previously reported bay-substituted
PBI-phenothiazine (PBI–PTZ) dyads in which PTZ is attached
to PBI through different orientations, different absorption spectra
were observed. For dyads with orthogonal geometry, PBI and PTZ
are completely electronically decoupled; the absorption spectrum
was similar to that of un-substituted PBI but for dyads with nonorthogonal geometry, the absorption spectrum was broad and
structureless and strong electronic coupling between PBI and
PTZ moieties was observed.57 Moreover, in the case of previously
reported bay substituted PBI–carbazole derivatives, red shifted
and broad structureless absorption spectra were observed, which
indicated that strong ground state electronic coupling was
present in the bay substituted PBI–carbazole derivatives due to
p-conjugation,56 while imide position substituted PBI–Cz dyads
(Scheme 1) inherit photophysical characteristics from the PBI.
The fluorescence of PBI–Cz dyads and the reference compound was studied (Fig. 1b). All the compounds show similar
emission wavelengths, and a structured emission peak appears
at 535 nm with a pronounced vibrational shoulder at 575 nm.
The emission of PBI–Cz dyads is quenched even in low polar
solvents such as toluene as compared to the reference compound PBI-Ph which is highly fluorescent (optically matched
solution was used). The fluorescence quantum yield (FF) of
PBI-Cz-2 is 0.44 (in toluene), which is lower than that of the
reference compound PBI-Ph (FF = 0.90). The linkers between
the PBI and the Cz moieties in PBI-Cz-1 and PBI-Cz-2 are
one and two phenyl rings respectively, and we observed the
different fluorescence quantum yield in these two dyads.
It should be noted that fluorescence is strongly quenched in
the case of PBI-Cz-1 (FF = 0.02) and PBI-Cz-3 (FF = 0.01) in
toluene (Table 1), and the photo-induced charge separation
(CS) is the possible reason for fluorescence quenching (the Cz
moiety as the electron donor). We proposed that the diﬀerence
in fluorescence quenching in PBI–Cz dyads is due to different
lengths of the spacer which controls the photo-induced electron transfer (PET) probability between Cz and PBI moieties.
As the length of the spacer increases, the electron transfer
probability between the two moieties decreases which results in
less fluorescence quantum yield in PBI-Cz-2 as compared to the
other two dyads. The solvent-dependent fluorescence of dyads
was also studied (see the ESI,† Fig. S16). In all solvents only
structured emission bands were observed in the case of PBI–Cz
dyads, and no CT emission band was observed. With increasing

The fluorescence lifetime of PBI–Cz dyads was also studied
(Fig. 2). In all the dyads the fluorescence emission decays
faster as compared to the reference compound and exhibits
biexponential features, which supports the PET process in
these dyads. For PBI-Ph, the decay of fluorescence at 520 nm
(LE emission) is with a lifetime of 4.3 ns, similar to that of the
unsubstituted PBI moiety which has a fluorescence lifetime of
4.4 ns (see the ESI,† Fig. S18).
For PBI-Cz-2 (Fig. 2b), the fluorescence decay is with a minor
biexponential character, with decay times of 0.6 ns (population
ratio 13%) and 3.9 ns (87%), respectively. For the dyads, PBI-Cz1 and PBI-Cz-3, showing weak fluorescence, the biexponential
decay feature is more significant. In the case of PBI-Cz-1 the
fluorescence lifetime is 0.1 ns (population ratio 82%) and 3.6 ns
(18%); while for PBI-Cz-3, the fluorescence lifetime is 0.6 ns
(population ratio 84%) and 4.3 ns (16%). The fast decaying
components of PBI-Cz-1 and PBI-Cz-3 generally indicate the
quenching channel of the 1LE state, e.g. photo-induced CS.58

The fluorescence decay of the PBI–Cz dyads was also studied in

The singlet oxygen quantum yields (FD) of the PBI–Cz
dyads were studied (Table 1), as a preliminary evolution of the
triplet state production ability of the compounds. The detailed
method for the measurement of FD values is given in the ESI.†
PBI–Cz dyads show very high singlet oxygen quantum yields in
toluene (FD = 0.72 for PBI-Cz-3). Since the PBI moiety is devoid of ISC
ability, the ISC is attributed to the charge transfer mediated triplet
state population in these dyads. It was found that by increasing the
‘effective’ spacer length in the dyads, the FD decreases. Moreover, in
polar solvents, the FD decreases sharply as well. In toluene, the
FD values are in the range of 38–72%, in dichloromethane, and the
PBI–Cz dyads showed FD of only 4–10%, whereas no singlet oxygen
generation was observed in acetonitrile. The solvent polarity dependency of FD indicates that the CT state is involved in the ISC, which
is the characteristic of the SOCT-ISC mechanism. It is interesting
that the Cz and the PBI p-conjugation planes do not adopt orthogonal geometry, but the ISC is efficient. This result is different
from previously proposed orthogonal molecular geometries for
electron donor-acceptor dyads showing efficient SOCT-ISC.46–48

Nanosecond transient absorption spectroscopy: triplet state
properties
Perylenebisimide (PBI) chromophore is devoid of ISC ability
and is highly fluorescent.61 However, PBI–Cz dyads show
significant singlet oxygen production (Table 1), which indicates
efficient ISC ability. For instance, the singlet oxygen quantum
yield (FD) of PBI-Cz-3 is 72% in toluene, for PBI-Cz-1 and
PBI-Cz-2, and the singlet oxygen quantum yields in toluene
are 45% and 38%, respectively. To study the triplet excited
states of the PBI–Cz dyads, nanosecond transient absorption
spectra were studied (Fig. 4). Upon 525 nm pulsed laser excitation, a prominent excited state absorption (ESA) band was
observed in the range of 400–650 nm which is similar to the
typical T1 - Tn absorption profile of the PBI moiety.62
For PBI-Cz-1, PBI-Cz-2 and PBI-Cz-3, ground state bleach
bands (GSB) were observed at 528 nm. To monitor the lifetime of
the triplet excited state, the decay traces at 470 nm were studied.
Interestingly, a long lifetime (110–189 ms) was observed in the case
of PBI–Cz dyads. The lifetime of the transient species detected in
the case of PBI–Cz dyads is greatly quenched in the presence of
air, which confirms the formation of triplet species. The PBIlocalized triplet state is much longer-lived than that observed
with a heavy atom effect in a Pt(II) complex (246 ns–1.0 ms).63,64
The triplet state lifetimes are also much longer than that
observed with the S1/Tn (n 4 1) energy level matching approach
(30–60 ms).65 However, in a PBI derivative with phenyl acetylide

Time-resolved EPR spectral study
In order to study the triplet state properties and the ISC
mechanisms of the dyads, time-resolved electron paramagnetic
resonance (TR-EPR) spectra of the dyads were recorded. Based
on the UV-Vis absorption spectrum (Fig. 1a), we acquired the
TR-EPR spectra of the compounds (Fig. 5) upon photoexcitation

at 355 and 530–536 nm, where the Cz (355 nm) and/or the PBI
(at 530 ca. and 355 nm) moieties show absorption maxima,
respectively.
Upon pulsed photoexcitation at 530–536 nm, all the dyads
show similar TREPR spectra, with eae/aea electron spin polarization (ESP, a and e refer to enhanced absorption or emission,
respectively). On the basis of the best-fit ZFS parameters,
D = ca. 46 mT and E = ca. 4 mT (Tables 4 and 5), the triplet
state is localized on the PBI moiety for all three dyads.
Polarization is not a typical triplet state accessed by a regular
SO-ISC,42 for its polarization would be eee/aaa (see the spectrum labeled PBI-ISC in Fig. 5a, simulated with the parameters
taken from ref. 42 and shown in Tables 4 and 5). The spectral
features are, instead, rather similar to those for compact PBI–
PTZ dyads, where the triplet was obtained by SOCT-ISC.42
Moreover, similar ESP patterns were observed in the case of

orbital charge transfer (SOCT-ISC) induced intersystem crossing in these dyads. The molecular conformation was varied by
changing the mutual orientation and the distance between the
perylenebisimide acceptor and the carbazole donor. Negligible
ground state electronic coupling between the PBI and the Cz
moiety was observed in all the dyads. High singlet oxygen
quantum yields (FD = 38 B 72%) were observed, especially in
the case of PBI-Cz-3 in which the donor and the acceptor are
separated by the smallest distance. All the dyads show the longlived PBI localized triplet state (tT is up to 190 ms), confirmed by
nanosecond transient absorption spectroscopy. It should
be noted that dyads (PBI-Cz-1 and PBI-Cz-3) with one phenyl
spacer between the donor and the acceptor adopt a nonorthogonal geometry, but the ISC is still efficient, which is
different from the previously reported molecular structural
profiles which require an orthogonal geometry for efficient
SOCT-ISC. Time-resolved electron paramagnetic resonance (TREPR)
spectroscopy reveals that all three dyads have the same electron
spin polarization of eae/aea, which is different from the SO-ISC
(eee/aaa), thus confirming the SOCT–ISC mechanism. Moreover, upon photoirradiation, PBI-Cz-1 and PBI-Cz-3 show the
formation of the near-IR absorbing PBI radical anion (PBI ) in
the presence of the sacrificial electron donor (TEOA). These
results are useful for future design of heavy atom-free triplet
photosensitizers and for the study of the charge recombination
in the electron donor/acceptor dyads.
